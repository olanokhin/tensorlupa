**In one sentence:** RoPE tells attention where each token stands by rotating its query and key by an angle that
depends on the position, so a head can see how far apart two tokens are.

## The problem it solves

[Attention](../attention/) compares the current [token](../token/) with earlier ones, but the comparison itself
knows nothing about order. Without extra help, "the dog bit the man" and "the man bit the dog" would look the same
to it. The model needs to know positions.

Early transformers added a separate list of numbers for each position to the token's embedding. RoPE does not add
anything to the [residual stream](../residual-stream/). It works inside attention, at the moment of comparison.

## How it works

1. A head's query is a list of 64 numbers. Split it into 32 pairs.
2. Treat each pair as a point on a plane and turn it by an angle. The angle is the token's position multiplied by
   the pair's own speed: the first pairs turn fast, the last ones very slowly.
3. Do the same with every key.
4. Compare query and key as usual.

When two rotated lists are compared, only the difference of their angles matters. So the match between a query at
position 60 and a key at position 57 depends on the distance, 3 tokens, and not on the numbers 60 and 57. A head
that learned "look at the previous token" does it the same way at the start of a story and at its end.

The fast pairs tell near positions apart, the slow ones far positions, like the hands of a clock.

## What it does not touch

- The values, the content that attention brings back, are not rotated. Position decides whom to look at, not what
  is taken.
- The residual stream carries no position marks.
- RoPE has no learned weights: the angles are fixed in advance.

## In micro-qwen3

- 4 heads of 64 numbers each, 32 rotating pairs per head.
- Applied to queries and keys in every layer, the same way as in Qwen3.
- The table of angles is computed once for all 256 positions of the context.

## A bit of history

RoPE was proposed by [Su and colleagues in 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864) and became the usual choice for
open language models, including the Qwen family.
