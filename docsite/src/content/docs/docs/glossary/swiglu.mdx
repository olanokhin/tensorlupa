**In one sentence:** SwiGLU is the way the [MLP](../mlp/) of each layer is built: the input is expanded twice,
once as a signal and once as a gate, and the gate decides how much of each signal passes.

## How it works

1. The MLP reads 112 numbers, a normalized copy of the [residual stream](../residual-stream/) (see
   [RMSNorm](../rmsnorm/)).
2. They are mixed into 336 numbers, twice, with two different sets of weights. One result is the **gate**, the
   other is the **signal**.
3. The gate goes through a smooth switch: a strongly negative number becomes almost zero, a positive one stays
   almost as it was.
4. Gate and signal are multiplied, position by position. These 336 products are the **neurons**.
5. The neurons are mixed back into 112 numbers, and the result is added to the stream.

The smooth switch in step 3 is called SiLU, or Swish, which is where the first half of the name comes from. The
second half, GLU, stands for "gated linear unit".

## Why a gate

In the older design a neuron had one input and a simple rule: pass if positive, block if negative. Here the
decision "does this neuron fire" and the amount "how strongly" come from two separate looks at the same input. The
model can detect a situation with one and choose the response with the other. In practice this gives better
quality for the same number of weights.

## A real step

The demo story is about to write " saw" after " and". At that step most neurons are nearly silent, and the deeper
the layer, the more of them take part:

| Layer | Neurons above 0.1, out of 336 | Strongest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 14 | 0.47 |
| 2 | 56 | 0.58 |
| 3 | 84 | 0.68 |

In the Lab, the large plates in the **Model** window are the neurons, 336 tiles each. Point at one to see what that

step of the MLP.

## In micro-qwen3

- 112 numbers in, 336 neurons, 112 numbers out, in each of 3 layers.
- Three sets of weights per layer: gate, signal and the way back, 112 × 336 each.
- The same construction as in Qwen3.

## A bit of history

The family of gated variants, SwiGLU among them, was compared by
[Shazeer in 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05202). It is now the usual MLP of open language models.
